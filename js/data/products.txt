Name Pure Egyptian Gold Magic Whitening Shower gel 
Description
It is a creamy shower cleanser intended to:

Cleanse the body and remove dirt/oil.
Help improve the appearance of dark spots and uneven skin tone.
Brighten dull-looking skin.
Leave the skin feeling softer and moisturized.
Be used particularly on areas such as the knees, elbows, thighs and other darker areas. 

Ingredients / key ingredients:

Kojic Acid – a skin-brightening ingredient commonly used to help reduce the appearance of pigmentation and dark spots.

L-Glutathione – an antioxidant marketed in this product for brightening/evening skin tone.

Glycerin/moisturizing base – helps reduce dryness and keep skin soft.

Vitamin C – listed by some sellers as an additional antioxidant/brightening ingredient.

Gold/golden extract or shimmer – appears to be included mainly for the product's cosmetic appearance/glow

Uses
It is intended for:

Daily body cleansing.

Helping improve the appearance of hyperpigmentation/dark spots.

Helping even the appearance of skin tone.

Brightening dull-looking body skin.

Cleansing areas prone to darker appearance, such as elbows and knees. 

What skin type is it suitable for?
Generally: normal, combination and oily body skin may tolerate it well.

Dry or sensitive skin: use more cautiously because brightening products containing kojic acid can sometimes cause irritation or dryness. Start 2–3 times per week, see how your skin reacts, and moisturize afterward.

For acne-prone body skin: it may help with the appearance of post-acne marks, but it isn't primarily an acne treatment.

How to use it
Apply to wet body skin, massage into a lather, leave for about 1–2 minutes, then rinse thoroughly. Some product listings recommend 2–3 minutes. 


I would not leave it on for a long time or use it repeatedly in an attempt to get faster whitening. Patch-test first, particularly if your skin is sensitive. Use body moisturizer afterward and SPF on exposed skin during the day.

Also, the "14 days" claim on the bottle should be viewed as a marketing claim, not a guarantee that your skin will lighten in exactly 14 days.
